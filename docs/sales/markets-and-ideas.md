# Markets and ideas (research, November 2026)

What the research found, which audiences fit WolofLink, and what to build or
try next. Sources are at the end; most are app-store pages and blogs, so treat
numbers as rough.

## What learners say about the big language apps

- **They like:** streaks and progress that build a habit; real listening and
  speaking; short lessons; working while commuting.
- **They complain about:** game mechanics crowding out learning; repetitive
  drills; speaking practice that "accepts any noise"; ads and paywalls that
  grow with every update; stories or dialogues removed in redesigns ("they
  were the best feature"); little help with real, fast speech.
- **What WolofLink does about it (0.24.0):** no ads, nothing paywalled at
  launch; hands-free listening mode; 6 dialogues; slow play on every line;
  test-out for people who already speak some Wolof; competitive features
  kept to friends only.

## Competition for Wolof specifically

A handful of small Wolof apps exist (a few ratings each, several with ads,
mostly iPhone, one paid phrase app with ~5 Android installs). None combines a
full path, offline audio, a holiday calendar, culture notes, organisations and
five interface languages. **The niche is open, but it's small: win it on
quality and trust (native-speaker review, real recordings).**

## Audiences, in order of fit

1. **Diaspora and heritage learners** (France, Italy, Spain, the UK, the US,
   Germany): second-generation Senegalese and Gambians who understand some
   Wolof. Several new African-language apps are built for exactly this
   group, and parents in particular. *WolofLink now has test-out and the five
   interface languages match where the diaspora lives.*
   Try: posts in diaspora associations and WhatsApp groups, mosques and
   dahiras abroad, Senegalese restaurants; a "learn with your parents" angle.
2. **NGO, embassy and development staff in Dakar** (energy, telecoms, NGOs and
   diplomacy are where most foreign professionals work). Peace Corps Senegal
   currently has no volunteer places, which leaves a gap for structured,
   self-paced Wolof before arrival. *Team codes and the organisations pack fit;
   pitch to HR and security/induction leads.*
3. **Oil and gas newcomers** (Sangomar and offshore gas since 2024; the AFOGEX
   expo was in Dakar in June 2026). Local-content rules mean mixed teams;
   French is the working language and English is rare, so a little Wolof
   goes a long way on site. *Pitch "site Wolof" for induction weeks.*
4. **Visitors to the Gambia** (Wolof is widely spoken there; UK charters and
   new routes from London and Birmingham, +15% forecast for 2026/27). *Store
   listing now names the Gambia; add the Gambia and Mauritania as countries.*
5. **Visitors to Senegal** (780,000 arrivals in 2023, target 3 million by
   2027, the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar, Diamniadio and Saly in
   November 2026). *Survival Wolof and the holidays calendar fit; don't use
   Olympic names or symbols anywhere.*
6. **Schools and universities abroad** with African-studies or study-abroad
   programmes in Dakar. *Organisations pack; offer a free class code.*

## Ideas for later (not built yet)

- **Real native recordings** of the most used lines (units 1–5 first), through
  the existing invited-recorder screen. Biggest quality gain.
- **Family mode for children** (picture-only lessons, no friends or accounts),
  aimed at diaspora parents. Needs a separate design for children's privacy
  rules before building.
- **Gambian Wolof notes** where words differ from Dakar usage, checked by a
  Gambian speaker.
- **Phrasebook printouts and audio packs** for organisations (Gumroad idea
  already in `gumroad-phrasebook.md`).
- **iPhone version** (the code is ready for it; needs an Apple developer
  account and an App Store review).
- **More A2 units** after the native speaker has checked units 21–24.
