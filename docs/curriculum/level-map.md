# WolofLink level map (CEFR-aligned)

This document maps the WolofLink course to the **Common European Framework of Reference for
Languages (CEFR)**, the European scale from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery), and explains the level
tests in the app. It is the basis for working towards outside recognition later (a university or
the Centre de linguistique appliquée de Dakar, CLAD).

> **Status.** This alignment is WolofLink's own, prepared from the course content and to be
> checked by our Wolof reviewers. It is **not** an official CEFR certification: no body certifies
> Wolof courses against the CEFR. Certificates say "CEFR-aligned by WolofLink" for that reason.
> The can-do statements below are written in our own words, following the CEFR's descriptions of
> each level.

## The levels at a glance

| Level | CEFR | In the app today | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1 · Beginner | **A1** | Complete (20 units) | 1–20 |
| Level 2 · Elementary | **A2** | Started (4 of 19 units) | 21–24 now; 25–39 planned |
| Level 3 · Intermediate | **B1** | Planned | 40–54 |
| Level 4 · Upper intermediate | **B2** | Future | 55 onwards |

Dialect: Senegalese (Dakar) Wolof in the official CLAD orthography. Gambian forms may be noted
alongside, never mixed into the main text.

## A1 · Beginner (units 1–20)

**Overall:** can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very simple sentences to
meet concrete needs; can introduce themselves and others, and ask and answer simple personal
questions, if the other person talks slowly and helps.

| Skill | The learner can… | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | recognise greetings, numbers, prices, days and times when spoken slowly and clearly | 1, 2, 7, 20 |
| Listening | follow short, simple directions and questions about themselves | 6, 15 |
| Reading | understand single words and short phrases on familiar topics (food, family, places) | 3, 4, 6, 13, 14 |
| Spoken interaction | greet people correctly, including the long Senegalese greeting exchange, and take leave | 1, 8, 17 |
| Spoken interaction | ask and answer simple questions (who, what, where, how much) | 5, 15 |
| Spoken interaction | buy food at the market and bargain with numbers and prices | 2, 3, 20 |
| Spoken interaction | accept and offer hospitality politely (teraanga) | 8, 17, 18 |
| Spoken production | describe themselves, their family, home, job and feelings in simple phrases | 4, 10, 11, 19 |
| Spoken production | say what they like doing and talk about free time | 16 |
| Writing | write familiar words and short phrases in the official spelling (ë, é, à, ñ, ŋ, long vowels) | all |
| Culture | know key etiquette: greetings, right hand, Ramadan, being a guest, main holidays | 12, 17 |
| Health | name parts of the body and say what is wrong at the doctor's | 9 |

**Overall:** can understand sentences and frequent expressions about immediate needs (shopping,

## B1 · Intermediate (planned units 40–54)

**Overall:** can deal with most situations while travelling or living in Senegal; can produce
simple connected text on familiar topics; can describe experiences, events, hopes and plans and
briefly give reasons and opinions.

| Skill | The learner can… | Planned units |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | understand the main points of everyday conversations, the radio and news on familiar topics | 54 |
| Spoken interaction | give opinions with reasons, compare choices, agree and disagree politely (opinions start at A2 in unit 37) | 48, 49 |
| Spoken interaction | host and visit with the right formulas; refuse politely | 50, 52 |
| Spoken production | tell a story or describe an event in the past, with sequence words | 46 |
| Spoken production | talk about hopes, plans and conditions ("if…") | 47 |
| Spoken production | give advice and explain rules, with reasons | 48 |
| Culture | use common proverbs appropriately; talk about faith, ceremonies, music and wrestling | 44, 45, 53 |

## The level tests (in the app)

Learners open them from **My progress → Take the A1 test / Take the A2 test**.

- **Questions:** 30 (fewer only if the level has fewer words), at least one from every unit of
  the level, the rest at random, each word once. A new mix every attempt.
- **Question types** rotate through five tasks: choose the Wolof for a meaning, listen and choose
  the meaning, type the Wolof, choose the meaning of a Wolof phrase, and build a sentence from
  word tiles. That covers listening, reading and writing.
- **Pass mark:** 80%. Mistakes don't end the test (no "drops"). Learners can retry any time.
- **Result:** the best score and the date of the first pass are saved and synced. Passing earns
  the level certificate, which then shows "Level test passed with N% (CEFR A1, aligned by
  WolofLink)". Learners who already speak some Wolof can pass the test without doing every unit.
- **Code:** `src/lib/levels.ts` (map, question choice, scoring), tested in `levels.test.ts`.

## What the tests don't measure yet (and the plan)

| Gap | Plan |
|---|---|
| Free speaking | A spoken part: record answers to prompts with the existing recorder; marked by a trained native-speaker examiner against a short rubric |
| Longer listening and reading | Add 2–3 graded texts or dialogues per level with comprehension questions |
| Free writing | A short message task (A2) and a short paragraph (B1), human-marked |
| Evidence that the cut-off is right | Pilot with teachers and learners: compare test scores with a teacher's judgement of the learner's level, then adjust the pass mark |
| Item quality | Native-speaker review of every test word (already part of the Wolof review), and removing items that almost everyone gets right or wrong |

## Towards outside recognition

1. Native-speaker review of all content, with reviewers credited (in progress).
2. This map and the tests checked by a qualified Wolof teacher; changes recorded here.
3. Pilot: 20–50 learners take the tests; publish pass rates and the teacher comparison.
4. Approach CLAD (UCAD) and a university that teaches Wolof with the map, the review method and
   the pilot results, asking for an endorsement or a jointly validated test.
5. Later: certificates issued with the partner; possibly a formal qualification through an
   awarding body.
